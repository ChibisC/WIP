# Ground beetle — *Pterostichus melanarius* (Carabidae)

## Size
- 12–19 mm (game: 16 mm).

## Anatomy and proportions
- Head .18 L, narrower than the pronotum, prognathous with **strong curved
  sickle mandibles** and long filiform 11-segment antennae (≈ .5 L).
- **Pronotum** .20 L, nearly square, slightly heart-shaped, wider than head,
  with fine margins and basal impressions.
- **Elytra** .55 L, together oval-elongate, each with **8–9 fine longitudinal
  striae** (ridged/grooved). Hind wings usually reduced (often flightless).
- Long slender legs (hind ≈ .85 L), tarsi 5-segmented; clearly a runner.

## Colour and surface
- **Glossy black** all over (legs black, sometimes reddish femora in var.
  rufipes); faint bluish/iridescent sheen on elytra in light.
  High clearcoat, low roughness, very subtle iridescence.

## Movement
- Fast **tripod-gait runner** with darting bursts; stops abruptly, antennae
  waving. Mostly **nocturnal**; hides under stones and litter by day.

## Behaviour
- Generalist predator (slugs, aphids, larvae, ants). Chases prey down.
- Defence: sprays foul-smelling secretion from pygidial glands at the abdomen
  tip; bites.
- Game: active pursuit predator; high armour (hard elytra); faster at night
  maps; retreats to cover when hurt.

## Facts for info cards
- Pterostichus melanarius is an important natural predator of slugs and pests in farmland.
- Ground beetles spray defensive chemicals from glands at the tip of the abdomen.
- Many carabids have fused elytra and cannot fly at all.
