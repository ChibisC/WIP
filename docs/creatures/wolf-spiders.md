# Wolf spiders — Lycosidae
Common predator: *Trochosa terricola* (ground wolf spider).
Boss: *Hogna carolinensis* (Carolina wolf spider) female carrying spiderlings.

## Size
- Trochosa terricola: female body 7–14 mm (game: 12 mm).
- Hogna carolinensis: female body 22–35 mm; leg span ~75 mm. Boss shown 1.5×.

## Anatomy and proportions (L = body length)
- **Cephalothorax** (prosoma) .48 L long, width .78 of its length; raised
  head region in front; fovea (groove) in the middle; slopes down at the back.
- **Abdomen** (opisthosoma) .52 L, oval, slightly narrower than the prosoma
  in males, larger in females; joined by a thin pedicel.
- **Eyes — 8 in 3 rows** (diagnostic): front row of 4 small eyes in a straight
  line low on the face; second row of 2 **very large forward-facing posterior
  median eyes**; third row of 2 medium posterior lateral eyes set back on top,
  facing sideways/back. Eyes glossy black with eyeshine.
- **Chelicerae** robust, hairy, dark, with fangs folded beneath; **pedipalps**
  short leg-like, held in front and moved constantly.
- **Legs** stout and hairy with spines; leg IV longest (≈ 1.35 L in Trochosa),
  then I, II, III (formula 4123). Segments: coxa, trochanter, femur, patella,
  tibia, metatarsus, tarsus with claws and scopula.

## Colour and surface
- Trochosa: dark brown with a pale broad median band on the carapace with two
  short dark lines (lanceolate) and thin pale lateral bands; abdomen brown with
  a lighter lanceolate heart mark; legs brown, faintly annulated.
- Hogna carolinensis: mottled grey-brown, dark brown carapace with pale
  midline stripe, underside black. Dense matte hair everywhere (sheen/fuzz).
- Matte, no clearcoat; eyes glossy.

## Movement
- **Alternating tetrapod gait**: L1, R2, L3, R4 move together, then R1, L2,
  R3, L4. Hydraulic leg extension at joints → quick bursts.
- Hunting pattern: walks a few steps, **stops motionless** (seconds), turns to
  face prey with the big eyes, creeps, then a **sprint and pounce**, pinning
  prey with the front legs and biting.
- Retreats to burrow/cover between hunts; does not build a capture web.

## Behaviour
- Females drag the round white/grey **egg sac attached to the spinnerets**;
  after hatching, **spiderlings ride on the mother's abdomen** for about a week.
- Defensive: raises front legs and opens chelicerae when cornered.
- Game (Trochosa): ambush-stalker; flees when below 30% hp.
  Boss (Hogna mother): phases — stalk/pounce → spiderlings scatter (minor
  enemies drop off) → frenzy sprints.

## Facts for info cards
- Wolf spiders' large eyes have a reflective layer — at night their eyeshine can be seen by torchlight.
- Mother wolf spiders carry their spiderlings on their backs for about a week after hatching.
- Wolf spiders hunt by sight and ambush rather than with webs.
