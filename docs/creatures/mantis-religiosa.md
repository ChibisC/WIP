# European mantis — *Mantis religiosa* (Mantidae)

## Size
- Female 70–90 mm, male 60–70 mm (game creature: 70 mm — huge relative to ants).

## Anatomy and proportions
- **Head triangular**, very mobile (swivels ~180°), large bulging compound
  eyes at the top corners with a dark "pseudopupil"; three ocelli (larger in
  males); thin filiform antennae ≈ .35 L.
- **Prothorax very long and slender** (≈ .30 L), widened at the base of the
  front legs.
- **Raptorial forelegs**: elongate coxa (≈ .14 L) with a **black eyespot with
  a white/yellow centre on the inner side** (diagnostic); femur thick with two
  rows of spines and a groove; tibia folds back into the femur like a
  jackknife, spined, ending in a hook. Held folded under the head in the
  "praying" pose.
- Meso- and metathorax short, carrying the wings and the two pairs of long
  thin walking legs (hind ≈ .65 L).
- Abdomen long (≈ .45 L), covered by folded wings that reach the tip in
  adults; tip with cerci.

## Colour
- Green (most common), brown or straw-yellow forms; wings slightly paler with
  a fine vein pattern; eyes same colour with darker pseudopupil. Satin finish.

## Movement and hunting
- Walks slowly on four legs with a jerky, **rocking/swaying** motion mimicking
  a leaf in the breeze; long periods of stillness; head tracks prey with
  small rotations; side-to-side "peering" sways to judge distance.
- **Strike**: forelegs shoot out and snap shut in ~50–100 ms, then pull prey
  to the mandibles; eats prey alive.
- **Deimatic display** when threatened: rears up, spreads wings and raises the
  forelegs to show the eyespots.

## Game
- Ambush predator: holds still (camouflaged), strikes anything entering reach
  with very high single-target damage, grabs and eats an ant (holding time).

## Facts for info cards
- A mantis can rotate its head almost 180° — few insects can do that.
- The black eyespot inside its forelegs is a key feature of Mantis religiosa.
- Mantises sway to judge distance: the motion gives them depth cues.
