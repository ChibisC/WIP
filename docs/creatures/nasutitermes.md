# Nasute Termites — *Nasutitermes corniger* (conehead termite)

Reference for the Nasute Termite colony. It covers real anatomy and behavior, what the game changes for gameplay, and what was corrected from the original design brief.

- **Species:** *Nasutitermes corniger* (Motschulsky). Blattodea: Termitidae: Nasutitermitinae. *N. costalis* is a junior synonym (Scheffrahn et al. 2005). The approved common name is **conehead termite** (older names: tree termite, coneheaded termite).
- **Range:** Neotropical (Mexico, Central and South America, West Indies). Introduced to the Papuan Region. Established in Dania Beach, Florida since 2001. Lives in urban, agricultural and natural tropical habitats.
- **Verified:** 2026-10-24 against the UF/IFAS publication EENY-745 (Tong, Tenn & Scheffrahn, revised 2024-11-13) and the FDACS Conehead Termite fact sheet (2024). Items marked **[genus]** come from a congener or from general termite biology, not from a *N. corniger* source. Items marked **[game]** are deliberate gameplay inventions.

---

## 1. Termites are not ants — anatomy shared by every caste

| Feature | Termite (build this) | Ant (do NOT build this) |
|---|---|---|
| Waist | **None.** The thorax joins the abdomen broadly, giving a soft, continuous, grub-like body. This is the single most important difference. | Narrow petiole/postpetiole waist |
| Antennae | **Straight, beaded** (moniliform): a chain of small bead-like segments. No elbow. | Elbowed (geniculate), long first segment |
| Cuticle | Workers and brood: thin, soft, slightly translucent. Abdomen shows faint segment bands, and dark gut contents (wood) show through the skin. | Hard, opaque |
| Legs | Six, **shorter and stockier** than an ant's: coxa, trochanter, femur, tibia, tarsus, claws | Longer, slimmer |
| Eyes | **Workers and soldiers have no eyes** and navigate by antennae, touch and chemical trails. Only reproductives (king, queen, alates) have compound eyes and ocelli. | Most castes have eyes |
| Development | Incomplete metamorphosis: egg → larva → worker, **or** larva/worker → presoldier → soldier. **No pupa.** | Egg → larva → pupa → adult |

**Antennal segments:** soldiers about 13 and reproductives about 15 **[genus]**, from the congener *N. longinasus*. *N. corniger* workers have **14** (a morphometric study of *N. corniger* castes in *Revista Brasileira de Entomologia*; the first worker instars show a short third segment that looks fused to the fourth).

## 2. Real castes of *N. corniger*

### Workers
- **Creamy-white**, wingless, **5–6 mm** long (FDACS).
- **Two sizes by sex:** large workers are female, small workers are male (UF/IFAS).
- They have reached a terminal molt and do all the chewing, building, feeding and grooming. They feed and groom soldiers and reproductives (FDACS).
- They have strong chewing mandibles. Their heads are slightly darker and more yellowish-tan than their bodies **[genus]**.
- **Development:** the same *Revista Brasileira de Entomologia* study (four colonies, Rio de Janeiro) found two worker lines: a larger line with four instars and a smaller line with three. Head sclerotization increases from instar to instar. All workers have a **pigmented (dark) intestine** except while moulting.
- Neither UF/IFAS nor the morphometric study describes worker head shape or colour in detail. The game's worker look (§8) follows the user's FIX brief.

### Soldiers (nasutes) — the only soldier type in this species
- About **5 mm** long (UF/IFAS, FDACS), slightly smaller than large female workers.
- **Head brown to black** (UF/IFAS), with a **cone-shaped forward projection, the nasus**, that squirts a defensive secretion.
- **Mandibles reduced to small points**, useless for biting (UF/IFAS).
- **Six setae (bristles) on the head.** This feature, together with polygyny, separates *N. corniger* from some other *Nasutitermes* (UF/IFAS).
- The head is swollen and rounded at the back because a large frontal gland fills it, and it tapers forward into the nasus. Seen from above it is pear- or teardrop-shaped **[genus]**.
- Soldiers make up **about 20% of the colony** (UF/IFAS).
- **Defense:** they squirt a noxious secretion from the nasus that deters ants and even anteaters (*Tamandua*) (Lubin & Montgomery 1981, cited by UF/IFAS). In nasutes the secretion is sticky and entangling (terpenoid) **[genus]**.
- Soldiers cannot feed themselves; workers feed them mouth to mouth **[genus]**.
- They develop through a **presoldier** stage: a soft, pale soldier-shaped molt with an untanned nasus that hardens and darkens **[genus]**.

### Reproductives
- **Alates** (winged adults): dark brown bodies with dark brown to black wings. Each wing has two thickened veins along its leading edge. Total length is **15–18 mm including wings**. Compound eyes and ocelli are present, and their size varies across the range (UF/IFAS).
- **Primary king and queen** develop from alates and keep **wing scales** (wing stubs), not "scars". They have dark brown, sclerotized head and thorax, compound eyes and ocelli.
  - Body length without wings is not given by the sources. The game uses **8.0 mm** (kit v2.1), about 1.45× the worker **[game estimate]**.
- **Queen:** the abdomen becomes hugely swollen (physogastric). Its intersegmental membrane stretches until the original dorsal plates (tergites) are only **small dark bars** spaced along the top of a pale, pleated abdomen. The membrane is ivory in young queens and turns yellow-amber to honey-brown with age (the user's queen photos; see "Royals as modelled" below) **[genus]**. She lays **up to about 3,000 eggs per day** and may live **up to 25 years** (FDACS).
- **Polygyny is common.** Some colonies have a single primary pair, but colonies with **multiple primary kings and queens** are common. **Adultoids** (alates that shed their wings inside the natal nest and stay to reproduce) have been counted **up to 33** (UF/IFAS).
- **Replacement reproductives:**
  - Second-form: wing pads, never winged, stay in the natal nest.
  - Third-form: develop from workers, have no wing buds, are unsclerotized, and have non-functional or absent eyes.
  - Both lay fewer eggs than primaries and usually appear after a primary dies or is cut off (UF/IFAS). **A real colony can survive losing its queen.**
- In older colonies the kings and queens can move between **satellite carton nests** joined by tubes. Satellites that become isolated can bud into new colonies (UF/IFAS).

### Brood
- **Eggs:** small, smooth, translucent cream ovals, kept in clusters **[genus]**.
- **Larvae:** tiny, pale, almost colorless miniature termites with large heads and short stubby legs. They grow and darken as they mature **[genus]**.
- UF/IFAS lists eggs and larvae as part of the colony.

## 3. Behavior

### Foraging
- **Soldiers go first.** Soldiers leave the nest at night, find food, and then recruit workers. Workers and soldiers forage on exposed surfaces at night (Traniello & Busher 1985, cited by UF/IFAS).
- **Trails become covered galleries.** Once a trail to food is established, the termites cover it with a soil/feces mixture (UF/IFAS).
  - The tubes are **about 5 mm wide or larger**, about one soldier body length. They are hollow, and a broken tube looks like straw.
  - The networks are **highly branched** and can reach **more than 30 m** from the nest. (The UF/IFAS page converts this as "~200 in", which is a typo: 30 m is about 100 ft.)
- Tubes run along walls, gates, cement blocks, stumps, fruit trees and the ground (FDACS). They are often more numerous and easier to see than the nests.
- Termites are surface feeders and cover their feeding sites with carton (UF/IFAS).
- **Antennae lead every movement**, tapping the ground and nestmates. Termites move in dense, often single-file columns and hug walls and edges **[genus]**.
- **Allogrooming and trophallaxis** (mouth-to-mouth feeding) are constant. Workers feed and groom soldiers and reproductives (FDACS).
- **Alarm:** termite soldiers drum or jerk their heads against the substrate to raise the alarm **[genus]**. This is not specifically documented for *N. corniger* in the sources.

### Colony life
- New colonies are founded by a pair of dealates after a swarm.
- Mature colonies swarm **once a year during the rainy season (May–November)**, usually after rain (UF/IFAS).
- A colony matures at **more than 50,000** workers and soldiers. Large colonies hold **800,000 to over 1.2 million** individuals (UF/IFAS). FDACS gives a typical size of about 900,000.

## 4. Nest

- **Material:** carton, **made primarily of feces** (UF/IFAS). FDACS describes it as chewed wood and feces bound with salivary secretions.
- **Look:** dark brown, papery, layered and laminated, with a lumpy outer surface **[genus/photos]**.
- **Structure:** a protective outer layer, a middle zone of interconnected galleries, and a **queen (royal) chamber near the center** (FDACS).
- **Location:** above ground at the base of trees, on trunks, or high in branches. Also ground nests, nests inside structures, and nests in trash piles, under cement blocks or in debris (UF/IFAS). Nests can be hidden under vegetation.
- **Polycalic:** older colonies have satellite carton nests linked by tubes (UF/IFAS, FDACS).

---

## 5. Diet

- **Wood and other cellulose**, not meat:
  - dry and wet wood, decayed wood, hard and soft wood
  - dead branches on living trees, dead palm leaf axils, bamboo
  - structural and non-structural lumber, furniture, fences, paper (UF/IFAS, FDACS)
- They prefer wood of intermediate density (FDACS).
- **Leaf litter is not listed** for this species in either source. The game therefore uses **wood** as the termite food resource.
- Gut contents are dark from wood and show through the workers' translucent abdomens.

## 6. Gameplay adaptations (real → game)

| Topic | Real *N. corniger* | Game |
|---|---|---|
| Soldier types | One soldier size | Two: **Nasute Soldier** (ranged, the real soldier) and **Major Soldier** (melee). The Major is **[game]**, borrowed from nasute species with two soldier sizes (e.g. *N. longinasus*). |
| Reproductives | Often several queens and kings; adultoids; replacement reproductives | **One king and one queen** in a fixed royal chamber (the user's call, 2026-10-25). When she dies, the colony is lost (game rule, matching the ant queen). |
| Secretion | Noxious, sticky, deters ants and anteaters | Nasute: a **ramping pure-damage** glue stream. The glue on targets is visual only. Major: a point-blank glue burst with a short hold. |
| Food | Wood and cellulose | **Wood** resource gathered from deadwood sources on maps |
| Foraging | Soldiers scout at night, workers follow, trails become tubes | Nasutes escort and screen workers. The AI sends soldiers ahead of worker columns. Covered galleries protect foragers. |
| Size | Worker 5–6 mm, soldier ~5 mm | **Worker 4.2 mm** (kit v2.2: the user's brief keeps the worker small and round so it reads differently from the 5.8 mm sickle-jaw worker), soldier 5 mm. Converted with the game's existing mm-to-world scale |
| Queen mobility | Can move between satellites in old colonies | Immobile in the royal chamber |
| Death | — | Termite corpses are food for ant colonies. Termites do not eat ant corpses (matches EotU). |

### Major Soldier design notes
- In the dimorphic congener *N. longinasus*, the major soldier's head is about **1.19× longer** (including nasus) and **1.36× wider** than the minor soldier's. Its nasus is proportionally about the same length.
- The game uses those size ratios, but gives the Major a **shorter, thicker nasus and a darker head** so it reads as a melee unit at a glance **[game]**.
- In *N. longinasus*, real majors actually have a paler head capsule with a darker nasus.

## 7. Corrections to the original design brief

| Original brief said | Sources say | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Worker ~4 mm | Workers 5–6 mm; large = female, small = male | v2.2: back to **~4 mm** (4.2 mm; male 3.9 / female 4.5) at the user's request, so it stays clearly smaller than the sickle-jaw worker. Flagged: smaller than FDACS's 5–6 mm |
| Nasute soldier 3.5–4 mm | Soldier ~5 mm | Nasute 5 mm (now a little longer than the workers) |
| Major 5 mm, king 6 mm | Not documented (Major is a game caste) | Major ~6.5 mm, king ~7 mm, all scaled from the corrected worker size |
| Soldier head dark reddish-brown to chestnut | UF/IFAS text: brown to black. The reference photos show glossy dark reddish-brown | Glossy dark reddish-brown (`#4A1E12`), lighter toward the edges and nasus tip (§8) |
| Royal "wing scars" | Wing scales (stubs) | Wing scales |
| Carton = chewed wood + saliva + feces | Primarily feces (UF); wood + feces + saliva (FDACS) | "Mostly feces, with chewed wood, bound with saliva" |
| Diet: wood, leaf litter, dead plant matter | Wood and cellulose; leaf litter not listed | Wood only |
| Soldiers form a perimeter around foragers | Soldiers find food first and recruit workers; trails are then covered | Keep the perimeter **and** add soldier-first scouting for the AI |
| Antennae ~13–15 segments | Workers 14 (*N. corniger* morphometrics); congener: soldier 13 | Soldier 13, worker 14, reproductives 15 |
| Brood: egg → larva → adult | Correct, but soldiers pass through a presoldier stage | Soldier brood tiles show a pale presoldier in the final growth stage |
| One queen; colony dies with her | Polygyny common; replacement reproductives exist | Kept as a game rule, flagged as a simplification |

## 8. Appearance used for the game model (from the user's reference photos)

### Royals as modelled (kit v2.1, 2026-10-25)

Rebuilt to the user's FIX spec (`claude/termite-royals/01-spec-royals-fix.md`). The queen's abdomen was checked against the user's two photos of physogastric queens: a mature amber queen in side view, and a pale young queen next to a yellow older one. The photos are study-only.
- **Real physogastric queens (photos):**
  - **Abdomen:** one soft, continuous, sausage-shaped body, not stacked rings. 9–10 pleated rolls; each roll's rear edge folds over the next. Creases are deep on the sides and shallow on top. Widest in the front-to-middle third, tapering to a rounded, slightly pointed rear. Flattened where it rests on the ground.
  - **Front:** the front rolls swell up around the back of the thorax, so the tiny head and thorax poke out from under them.
  - **Plates:** the old tergites remain only as **small dark bars** on top of each roll near its crease. Underneath are smaller, paler sternites, and there is a spiracle dot on each side of each roll.
  - **Surface:** glossy, wet and translucent. The membrane is **pale ivory in young queens and yellows to amber and honey-brown with age**, darker in the creases.
- **Model:**
  - **Queen abdomen:** 22 mm long (6× head + thorax), 4.9 mm wide (3.5× head width). Default age 0.5 (yellow-amber), adjustable per queen.
  - **Kings:** 8.0 mm (1.45× worker) with a modest abdomen (1.25× head + thorax).
  - **Colours:** head and thorax `#2E1E14`, abdominal plates `#3E2A1C` with pale membrane `#C9AE88`, legs and antennae `#7A5636`. Dark like the species' alates ("dark brown bodies with dark wings", UF/IFAS).
  - **Royal anatomy:** triangular wing-scale stubs angled back from the sides of the meso- and metathorax, smoothly bulging faceted compound eyes, two ocelli, saddle pronotum.
- **One queen:** *N. corniger* colonies are often polygynous (UF/IFAS: "colonies with multiple primary kings and queens are common"), but the game keeps **one queen** per colony (the user's call, 2026-10-25). She uses the kit's default age, 0.5 (yellow-amber).

### Workers and soldiers

The user supplied four reference photos (kept in the model kit's `refs/`):
- specimen photos of a nasute soldier: top, side, and a close-up of the head in side view
- a live macro photo of soldiers
- top views of a soldier and a worker

These photos read warmer than the UF/IFAS wording, so the model follows them.

- **Soldier head:**
  - a pear/teardrop with a smooth, bulbous rounded back
  - tapers continuously into a straight, sharply pointed cone; the nasus is roughly as long as the bulb
  - side view: the top line slopes straight down to the tip and the underside is slightly concave
  - colour: glossy dark reddish-brown to chestnut, lighter and translucent toward the edges and nasus tip, with a strong highlight
  - a few long, thin, pale bristles on the top and sides
- **Soldier proportions** (fraction of body length L, antennae excluded):
  - lengths: head including nasus about 0.38 (nasus about 0.15–0.17), thorax about 0.13–0.17, abdomen about 0.37–0.45
  - widths: head about 0.22, thorax about 0.13, abdomen about 0.25–0.28
  - antennae about 0.4 L
  - side view: the head's back rises above the thorax, and the abdomen top sits higher than the thorax
- **Soldier body:**
  - warm amber-orange, waxy and slightly translucent
  - each tergite darkens toward its rear margin, with a lighter translucent band between segments
  - a soft dark gut patch shows through the skin
  - sparse long pale hairs along the rear edge of each tergite
- **Soldier thorax:** three narrow plates (a saddle-shaped pronotum with a raised front lobe, then meso- and metanotum), joined broadly to both head and abdomen. There is no waist.
- **Soldier legs and antennae:**
  - amber-orange and translucent
  - legs come out under the body: femurs angle up and out, tibiae down to the ground, so the body is held off the ground
  - tibial spurs and claws
  - antennae of about 13 beads that lengthen slightly toward the tip, held forward with a gentle curve
- **Worker (kit v2.2, 2026-10-25):** small, compact and round, so it can't be mistaken for the sickle-jaw worker at any zoom.
  - **Size:** 4.2 mm, stocky.
  - **Head:** a high rounded dome, pale straw `#C9A266`, smooth and glossy, with **faint** low-contrast sutures (no white lines).
  - **Legs and antennae:** short, sturdy and pale cream; short beaded antennae with 14 articles.
  - **Abdomen:** a plump rounded oval, creamy and translucent, with soft amber bands and a dark gut patch.
  - **Mouthparts:** small chewing mandibles under the head front; no eyes.
  - The user's worker photo (top view) shows a darker brown head with a pale Y suture. The brief chose the paler straw dome so the two species' workers read differently; this is a game choice.
  - Before v2.2: 5.5 mm, a rounded amber-brown head (`#9A6430`) with a bright pale Y suture, the same head as the sickle-jaw worker.
