# Devil's coach horse — *Ocypus olens* (Staphylinidae, rove beetles)

## Size
- 20–32 mm (game: 26 mm).

## Anatomy
- Long, parallel-sided body; large square head (≈ .17 L) as wide as the
  pronotum with **large curved mandibles**; short antennae (≈ .3 L).
- Pronotum ≈ .15 L, slightly narrowed behind.
- **Very short elytra** covering only the first abdominal segments (≈ .15 L),
  leaving a long, flexible, segmented **exposed abdomen** (≈ .5 L) with
  two small pale-tipped glands at the end.
- Long-ish running legs; fore tarsi widened.

## Colour
- **Matte black**, finely punctured, covered in short dull hair; no gloss.

## Movement
- Fast scuttling runner; abdomen flexes side to side.

## Behaviour
- Nocturnal predator in leaf litter.
- Threat display: **raises the abdomen up and forward like a scorpion**,
  opens mandibles wide and can release a foul odour from abdominal glands;
  bites painfully.
- Game: aggressive predator with a display telegraph before a powerful bite.

## Facts for info cards
- The devil's coach horse curls its tail up like a scorpion, though it has no sting.
- It releases a foul-smelling chemical from glands at the abdomen tip when threatened.
