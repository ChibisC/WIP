# Brown stone centipede — *Lithobius forficatus* (Lithobiomorpha)

## Size
- 18–30 mm (game: 25 mm).

## Anatomy
- **Flattened** body; head with long many-segmented antennae (≈ .4 L) and
  ocelli clusters on the sides; **forcipules** (modified first-trunk legs) curve
  under the head as venom claws.
- Trunk with **15 leg-bearing segments** in adults → **15 pairs of legs**,
  one pair per segment; tergites alternate long/short (diagnostic of
  Lithobius), giving a slightly irregular plate pattern.
- Legs increase in length toward the rear; last pair longest, trailing, used
  as sensory/defensive "tail".

## Colour and surface
- Glossy **chestnut to reddish-brown**, slightly paler legs; shiny dorsal
  plates (clearcoat), satiny legs.

## Movement
- **Metachronal leg waves** passing from back to front along each side, the
  two sides in antiphase; body undulates slightly at speed.
- Very fast, darting runs between cover; freezes; flees into crevices.

## Behaviour
- Nocturnal ambush/active hunter of small arthropods; seizes with forcipules
  and injects venom.
- Defence: can shed (autotomise) legs; rear legs pinch.
- Game: fast striker, venom bite, darts into cover when hurt.

## Facts for info cards
- Lithobius hatch with only 7 pairs of legs and add more at each moult until they reach 15.
- A centipede's venom claws are actually its first pair of legs, modified into fangs.
