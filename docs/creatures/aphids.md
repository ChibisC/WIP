# Aphids — *Aphis fabae* (black bean aphid) and *Acyrthosiphon pisum* (pea aphid)

## Size
- A. fabae 1.5–2.6 mm; A. pisum 2.5–4 mm (game: 2 mm and 3 mm).

## Anatomy
- Soft **pear-shaped** body, head small with antennae (A. pisum antennae
  longer than body; A. fabae ≈ .7 L).
- Mouthparts a long **rostrum** (beak) pressed into the plant stem.
- Paired **siphunculi (cornicles)** — tubes sticking up/back from the rear of
  the abdomen; a short tail-like **cauda** at the tip.
- Six slender legs; most adults wingless (apterous); colonies of all ages.

## Colour
- A. fabae: matte **black to dark olive** with waxy whitish patches.
- A. pisum: translucent **pale green**, glossy, darker green gut.
- Nymphs paler and smaller.

## Behaviour
- Live in dense clusters on stems, nearly immobile, feeding on phloem.
- Excrete sugary **honeydew** droplets from the anus. When an ant strokes them
  with its antennae they release a droplet for it to drink.
- Kick and wave the hind legs and release alarm pheromone from the cornicles
  when attacked. Reproduce parthenogenetically (clusters grow).
- Game: aphid cluster = renewable honeydew node; Lasius bonus when tended;
  ladybird/predator threats not needed — rival ants can steal clusters.

## Facts for info cards
- Many aphids are born pregnant: embryos already contain developing embryos (telescoping generations).
- Ants protect aphids and in return "milk" them for honeydew.
