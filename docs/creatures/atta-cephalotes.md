# Leafcutter ant — *Atta cephalotes* (Myrmicinae, Attini)

## Castes (most polymorphic ants in the game)
- **Minim** 1.5–2.5 mm (head width ~0.8 mm): garden tending, brood care,
  hitchhike on leaf fragments.
- **Media/forager** 5–8 mm: cut and carry leaves.
- **Major/soldier** 12–16 mm: enormous head, defence of trails and nest.
- Queen ~20–22 mm with huge gaster and bulky mesosoma.

## Anatomy and proportions
- Media (L = 7 mm): head .30 L, broad and **heart-shaped** (two rounded
  posterior lobes with a median notch), small occipital spines; eyes small,
  set forward. Antennae **11 segments**, long scape reaching the occiput.
- Major (L = 14 mm): head .40 L and wider than long, with pronounced lobes;
  mandibles massive and sharp-edged (can slice skin), dark-tipped.
- Minim (L = 2 mm): head .26 L, rounded, proportionally large eyes.
- Mesosoma .30 L with **three pairs of spines**: long anterolateral pronotal
  spines, paired mesonotal spines, and propodeal spines; surface tuberculate.
- Waist: petiole + postpetiole (two nodes), .10 L.
- Gaster small, .26 L, roughened.
- Legs very long: hind leg ≈ 1.2 L.

## Colour and surface
- Rusty red-brown to orange-brown, matte and rough (tubercles, short hooked
  hairs). Majors of A. cephalotes have a glossier, sparsely hairy head.
  Mandible tips and spine tips darker.

## Behaviour
- **Leaf cutting**: anchors with the hind legs on the leaf edge and pivots,
  scissoring one mandible while vibrating (stridulating) to stiffen the leaf,
  producing a roughly **semicircular** cut sized to the worker.
- **Carrying**: fragment gripped in the mandibles and held **upright above
  the head**, often taller than the ant. Minims ride on fragments to fend off
  parasitic phorid flies.
- Long cleared trunk trails; colonies of millions.
- **Fungus garden**: leaves are cleaned, chewed to pulp and planted with the
  cultivar *Leucoagaricus gongylophorus*, forming a spongy greyish-white
  comb. The fungus (gongylidia swellings) feeds larvae and queen.
  → Species economy: leaves → fungus garden → food.
- Refuse is dumped in separate chambers tended by old workers.
- Defence: majors bite; workers have a weak sting (not used in fights).

## Facts for info cards
- Leafcutters farmed fungus long before humans farmed anything — for about 50 million years.
- A mature Atta nest can contain millions of workers and extend several metres deep.
- Minims ride on leaves to guard carriers from phorid flies that lay eggs in ant heads.

**Mandibles**: triangular blades, the masticatory margin with 7–8 teeth, the apical tooth long and hooked, the
teeth darkening to near black. Media: like a broad formicine triangle but stronger and more curved. Soldier and
major: thick, broad, strongly curved shears (photos: dark red-brown to near black, a row of ~8 black teeth along a
long cutting edge, the closed pair crossing).

**Colour and surface** (photo samples, approximate sRGB): media and soldier rusty orange-brown (#8a4424–#9a5030),
matte and finely rough with tubercles and short hooked golden hairs; minim paler orange-tan (#b07040); major head
deep chestnut-red and glossy (#7a3018 → #5c2a16 at the front) with a woolly red hair tuft on each side of the frontal
area, mandibles near black at the teeth; queen dark chestnut (#5a2a16) with golden hairs and a satin gaster.

Where the photos disagree: some majors in the photos are A. sexdens/laevigata (dull, densely hairy heads); the model
follows A. cephalotes (shining head lobes, the hair only as the frontal tufts).
