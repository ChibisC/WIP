# Ants (Formicidae) — shared anatomy, gait and behaviour

## Body plan
- **Head**: prognathous (mouthparts forward). Compound eyes on the sides at about
  mid-length; three ocelli on the vertex in queens, males and some workers
  (Formica). Clypeus in front, frontal carinae between the antennal sockets.
- **Antennae**: geniculate (elbowed). Long first segment (**scape**, roughly the
  head length) then the **funiculus** (pedicel + flagellomeres). Worker/queen
  antennae have 12 segments in most species (scape + 11), 11 in Atta,
  10 in Solenopsis (with a 2-segmented club). Males have 13 and a short scape.
  At rest the scape points sideways/back along the head and the funiculus
  points forward and down.
- **Mandibles**: usually triangular blades with a toothed inner (masticatory)
  margin that close across each other in front of the clypeus. Exceptions:
  falcate hooks in Eciton soldiers, massive shearing jaws in Atta majors.
- **Mesosoma** (thorax + fused first abdominal segment, the propodeum):
  pronotum, mesonotum, propodeum with spiracle; worker profile low and
  continuous; queen mesosoma tall and bulky (flight muscle) with wing scars.
- **Waist**: formicines (Lasius, Formica) have a single **petiole** shaped as an
  upright scale; myrmicines (Solenopsis, Atta) and Eciton have **petiole +
  postpetiole** (two nodes). The petiole has a short anterior peduncle.
- **Gaster**: smooth oval of 4–5 visible segments. Formicines end in a round
  **acidopore** fringed with hairs (no sting); myrmicines and army ants have a
  retractable **sting**.
- **Legs**: three pairs on the mesosoma. Coxa, trochanter, femur, tibia, tarsus
  of 5 segments ending in paired claws. Fore tibia has a large comb-like spur
  (strigil) opposite a notch on the basitarsus — the antenna cleaner.
  Typical segment share of leg length: coxa .14, trochanter .05, femur .32,
  tibia .28, tarsus .21. Fore legs ≈ .80 and mid legs ≈ .90 of hind leg length.
  Coxae of fore legs hang forward under the pronotum; mid and hind coxae sit
  close together further back, so the hind legs splay strongly backwards.

## Gait and movement
- **Alternating tripod**: L1 + R2 + L3 swing while R1 + L2 + R3 support, then
  swap. Duty factor ≈ 0.5–0.6 when walking, lower when running. Feet plant and
  stay planted; the body advances over them. Faster walking raises stride
  frequency (Lasius up to ~15 Hz) and slightly lengthens the stride; it is
  never a slide.
- Body pitch follows the ground; a slight vertical bob at twice the stride
  frequency. Head and antennae turn toward stimuli before the body does.
- Movement is intermittent: short runs, brief stops to antennate the ground,
  re-orientation. Searching ants zigzag (correlated random walk); trail
  followers walk straight with antennae low and sweeping across the trail.

## Behaviours to show
- **Antennation**: meeting nestmates touch antennae and inspect each other.
- **Trophallaxis**: two ants face to face, mandibles open, mouths together,
  antennae drumming on each other while liquid food is passed.
- **Grooming**: pulling each antenna through the fore-leg strigil; rubbing the
  legs together; cleaning the gaster with the hind legs.
- **Brood care**: carrying eggs (clumps), larvae and pupae in the mandibles;
  piling brood by stage; feeding larvae; moving brood to better chambers.
- **Carrying**: load held in the mandibles, head raised, body slightly tilted
  up at the front. Group transport of large items is jerky — ants pull and
  push at different moments.
- **Alarm**: mandibles open wide, antennae up and forward, gaster raised
  (myrmicines bend the sting forward), running in loops toward the stimulus.
- **Fighting**: biting legs/antennae and holding on; mandible-to-mandible
  wrestling; several ants spread-eagle an enemy by pulling its appendages in
  opposite directions, then drag it off.
- **Death**: legs fold under and curl, antennae curl, the body tips onto its
  side or back. Workers carry corpses to a refuse area (necrophoresis,
  triggered by oleic acid from decomposition).
- **Digging**: biting off soil pellets with the mandibles, raking with the
  fore legs, walking backwards out of the gallery with the pellet, dropping it
  on a spoil heap outside or in a refuse chamber.
