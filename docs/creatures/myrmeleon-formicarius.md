# Antlion larva — *Myrmeleon formicarius* (Myrmeleontidae)

## Size
- Mature larva 10–15 mm (game: 14 mm); pit 25–50 mm across, ~25 mm deep.

## Anatomy
- **Plump, broad, flattened oval abdomen** much wider than the small head
  and thorax; body covered in forward-directed **bristles** that anchor it in
  sand.
- Small flat head bearing **huge sickle-shaped jaws** (mandible + maxilla
  forming a hollow sucking tube), each with 3 inner teeth; jaws as long as the
  head + thorax; small eye tubercles at the jaw bases.
- Short legs (hind legs fused tarsi), adapted for walking **backwards**.

## Colour
- Mottled grey-brown/sandy with darker speckles; jaws reddish-brown. Matte,
  dusty, hairy.

## Behaviour
- Digs a **conical pit** in loose dry sand by walking backwards in spirals and
  flicking sand out with the head; the pit walls sit at the angle of repose.
- Waits **buried at the bottom** with only the jaws exposed, open wide.
- When an ant slips in, it **flicks sand at the prey** to cause small
  avalanches so it slides down, seizes it, injects digestive enzymes and
  sucks it dry, then flicks the empty husk out of the pit.
- Game: static trap hazard; pit slows and drags ants to the centre; the larva
  can be dug out and killed by swarming (then it is food).

## Facts for info cards
- An antlion larva has no anus; waste is stored until it pupates.
- Antlion pits are built at the angle of repose — any extra weight makes the wall collapse.
- The adult is a delicate winged insect resembling a damselfly.
